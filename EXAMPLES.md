# 📚 Example Analyses

Real-world examples of business ideas analyzed by The Devil's Advocate.

## Example 1: Food Delivery App

### Input

**Business Name:** QuickBite Local

**Description:**
A food delivery app focused exclusively on local, independent restaurants 
(no chains). We charge restaurants 15% commission instead of the 30% that 
DoorDash charges. Our value prop is supporting local businesses.

**Target Market:**
Conscious consumers aged 25-45 who prefer local restaurants over chains. 
Initial launch in Austin, TX. 50,000 potential users in launch area.

**Revenue Model:**
- 15% commission per order
- Average order value: $35
- Projected: 1000 orders/month by month 6

**Key Assumptions:**
- Restaurants will switch due to lower fees
- Consumers will download a new app to support local
- Can achieve price parity with incumbents
- Delivery drivers will work for us at same rates

**Competitive Landscape:**
DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub all in market. Our advantage is local focus 
and lower fees.

### Analysis Result

**VERDICT: DEAD ON ARRIVAL**

You've built a business model where your primary value proposition 
(lower restaurant fees) directly funds your main competitor's ability 
to destroy you. You're trying to out-execute billion-dollar companies 
with 10% of their revenue per transaction.

**THE KILL SHOT:**

Unit economics collapse under driver costs. If you're charging 15% on 
a $35 order ($5.25), and the driver needs $8-12 per delivery to make 
it worthwhile, you lose money on every single transaction. DoorDash 
charges 30% because they have to—not because they're greedy.

**SCENARIO A (The Market):**

Month 3: You've convinced 30 local restaurants to join. Sounds good until 
you realize customers won't download a 4th delivery app for the 8 restaurants 
they actually order from. Your app has Thai, Ethiopian, and Vietnamese options, 
but the customer wants pizza. They go back to DoorDash. Your activation rate 
is 12%.

**SCENARIO B (The Operations):**

You launch with contracted drivers. They realize they make 40% more per hour 
on DoorDash due to higher volume (more orders = less wait time). Your drivers 
multi-app, prioritizing DoorDash orders. Your delivery times balloon to 60+ 
minutes. Restaurants start complaining. Three of your best partners quit after 
bad reviews.

**SCENARIO C (The Black Swan):**

DoorDash notices you're signing up their restaurant partners in Austin. They 
launch a "Local Love" campaign, temporarily dropping fees to 15% for independent 
restaurants in Austin only, funded by their $2B war chest. They can lose money 
in Austin forever. You cannot. You're dead in 90 days.

**THE STEEL MAN ARGUMENT:**

The ONLY path: Don't be a delivery app.

Instead:
1. Build a "Switch Kit" SaaS tool that helps restaurants leave DoorDash
2. License it to restaurants for $199/month
3. Integrate with existing delivery services or restaurant-owned drivers
4. Revenue from SaaS, not transaction fees
5. Partner with a driver co-op or use restaurant staff
6. Launch in 5 cities simultaneously to avoid local targeting
7. Your moat is the switching software, not the marketplace

Validate this pivot by:
- Selling the SaaS tool FIRST (before building delivery)
- Get 20 restaurants paying $199/month
- If they won't pay for the tool, they won't switch delivery platforms

## Example 2: AI Resume Builder

### Input

**Business Name:** ResumeAI Pro

**Description:**
An AI-powered resume builder that uses GPT-4 to write professional resumes 
based on a 10-minute interview with the user. We guarantee ATS-compatible 
formatting and offer unlimited revisions.

**Target Market:**
Job seekers, primarily recent graduates and career changers. 10M+ US job 
seekers per year. Targeting 0.1% market share = 10,000 customers.

**Revenue Model:**
- One-time payment: $49 per resume
- Premium: $99 (includes cover letter and LinkedIn optimization)
- Annual revenue goal: $500k (5,000 customers at $100 avg)

**Key Assumptions:**
- ATS compatibility is a major pain point
- People will pay $49-99 for AI-written resume
- Can rank in Google for "resume builder"
- User interview provides enough context for quality output

**Competitive Landscape:**
- Canva: Free, design-focused
- Resume.io: $24.95/month subscription
- LinkedIn: Free resume builder
- Hundreds of templates available free online

### Analysis Result

**VERDICT: RISKY**

You're selling a $49 solution to a problem that free and $5 alternatives 
already solve, in a market where customers make one purchase ever and never 
come back. Your customer acquisition cost will eat you alive.

**THE KILL SHOT:**

Customer acquisition economics. To rank in Google organically for "resume builder" 
requires 12-18 months and $100k+ in content/SEO. To run Google Ads profitably, 
you need customer acquisition cost under $40. Resume-related keywords cost $8-15 
per click, with 3-5% conversion rate. That's $160-500 CAC for a $49 product. 
You lose money on every customer.

**SCENARIO A (The Market):**

Month 6: You've gotten 200 customers through your network and early marketing. 
Conversion rate is 2%. CAC is $180. You're burning money. You pivot to SEO. 
Month 12: You rank on page 3 for "ai resume builder." You get 50 visits/month, 
convert 2 customers. You need 400 customers/month to hit goals. You won't get 
there for 3+ years.

**SCENARIO B (The Operations):**

The AI works great—too great. Users realize they can use ChatGPT directly for 
free with the same prompts. A Reddit post titled "I got ResumeAI Pro quality 
for free using this ChatGPT prompt" goes viral. Your competitive moat evaporates. 
You're now competing with free.

**SCENARIO C (The Black Swan):**

LinkedIn releases "Resume AI" as a free feature for all users. It auto-populates 
from their profile (better data than your 10-minute interview). It's ATS-compatible 
(they have ATS partnerships). It's free. Your target market disappears overnight.

**THE STEEL MAN ARGUMENT:**

Narrow and deepen. Don't compete in the broad resume market.

Instead:
1. Pick ONE industry vertical (e.g., software engineers, nurses, teachers)
2. Build industry-specific expertise (templates, keywords, hiring manager insight)
3. Charge $299, not $49 (premium positioning)
4. Add career coaching: resume + mock interview + job search strategy ($999 package)
5. Build a referral engine: Successful placements refer friends
6. Partner with bootcamps/universities (B2B channel, they pay $150/student)

Validate with:
- Sell 50 resumes manually at $299 in your chosen vertical
- Achieve 30%+ interview rate for clients (track and publish this metric)
- Get 10+ testimonials with job titles and companies
- Calculate CAC via partnerships before building tech

If you can't sell 50 manually at $299, the AI won't help.

## Example 3: SaaS for Plumbers

### Input

**Business Name:** PlumberOS

**Description:**
All-in-one business software for plumbing companies. Includes scheduling, 
invoicing, customer management, and route optimization. Mobile app for 
technicians. Replaces 4-5 separate tools plumbers currently use.

**Target Market:**
Small plumbing companies (2-20 employees) in the US. 120,000 plumbing 
companies in the US, 70% are small businesses = 84,000 potential customers.

**Revenue Model:**
- $199/month per company
- Annual contracts with 10% discount
- Target: 500 customers in year 1 = $100k MRR

**Key Assumptions:**
- Plumbers currently use 4-5 separate tools
- They'll pay $199/month for integrated solution
- Can reach them via Google Ads and trade shows
- Churn rate under 5%/month

**Competitive Landscape:**
- ServiceTitan: $500-1000/month, focused on larger companies
- Jobber: $99-299/month, generalist field service
- Housecall Pro: $49-249/month, similar offering
- QuickBooks + spreadsheets: What most use now

### Analysis Result

**VERDICT: VIABLE**

This could work if you execute extremely well on sales and customer success. 
The market exists, the pain is real, and the economics work. But you're 
entering a competitive market where switching costs are high and sales 
cycles are long.

**THE KILL SHOT:**

Implementation hell causes churn before you break even. A plumbing company 
needs to migrate years of customer data, train technicians who hate new 
software, and risk their business operations during the switch. If 
implementation takes more than 2 weeks or fails once, they churn. You 
need 8-10 months to break even on CAC. Churn before breakeven kills you.

**SCENARIO A (The Market):**

Month 8: You've signed 60 customers through aggressive outbound sales. 
Acquisition cost is $1,500 (demos, sales calls, implementation). Looks good. 
Month 10: 15 customers have churned (25%). Why? "Too complicated," "My guys 
won't use it," "Went back to what worked." Your LTV is now $4,000. 
LTV:CAC is 2.7x—survivable but not thriving. You need to reduce churn or 
you'll never scale profitably.

**SCENARIO B (The Operations):**

Your product is good, but each customer needs customization. Company A wants 
integration with their specific parts supplier. Company B needs a custom 
report for their bank. Company C requires bilingual interfaces. You spend 
60% of engineering time on custom requests. Your product roadmap stalls. 
New feature development stops. You become a consulting company with SaaS 
pricing.

**SCENARIO C (The Black Swan):**

Housecall Pro, recently acquired by Vista Equity Partners ($1B+ fund), drops 
prices to $99/month for a limited time and launches a plumber-specific version. 
They can afford to lose money for 18 months to gain market share. You can't. 
You either match their pricing (and burn cash) or lose deals. The private 
equity playbook is to consolidate this market. You're in the way.

**THE STEEL MAN ARGUMENT:**

Niche further and nail implementation.

Execute this way:
1. Focus on ONE plumbing sub-niche first: Residential service plumbers (not new construction)
2. Build the absolute best implementation process in the industry:
   - Data migration done FOR them in 48 hours
   - On-site training included
   - First month "success guarantee" or full refund
3. Charge $299/month (premium price = better customers, higher LTV)
4. Target $2,000 CAC, 10-month payback, $30k LTV (via upsells)
5. Add revenue features: Online booking ($49/mo extra), customer financing ($99/mo extra)
6. Build community: Monthly plumber mastermind calls, best practices sharing
7. Expand to adjacent trades ONLY after 200+ plumber customers with <3% monthly churn

Validate before building:
- Interview 50 plumbing companies about their biggest software pain
- Pre-sell to 10 companies ($199/mo for lifetime early adopter rate)
- Manually manage their ops for 90 days to learn the workflow
- Build the software based on those 10 companies' real needs

Don't build for an imaginary plumber. Build for 10 real ones.

Success metrics:
- Month 3: 10 pilot customers, <10% churn
- Month 12: 100 customers, <5% churn, $300k ARR
- Month 24: 300 customers, <3% churn, $1M+ ARR

## Key Patterns Across Examples

### Why Ideas Fail

1. **Unit Economics Don't Work**: Revenue per customer < cost to acquire them
2. **No Defensible Moat**: Competitors can easily copy or undercut
3. **Implementation is Harder Than It Looks**: Customers won't actually switch
4. **Free Alternatives Exist**: Why pay when good-enough is free?
5. **Market is Too Broad**: Trying to serve everyone serves no one well

### What "Viable" Ideas Have

1. **Specific Target Customer**: Not "young people," but "women aged 25-34 in Austin"
2. **Clear Path to First 10 Customers**: How will you get them without paid ads?
3. **Reasonable Unit Economics**: LTV > 3x CAC at minimum
4. **Narrow Initial Focus**: One problem, one customer type, nail it
5. **Validation Plan**: How to test assumptions before building

### How to Use These Examples

1. Compare your idea to these patterns
2. Identify which failure mode is most likely for you
3. Apply the "Steel Man" pivot framework
4. Validate before building
